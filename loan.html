Loan Scams

What is Loan Scams?

Unsolicited offers of loans or loan services made under false pretences are a common component of loan scams. Through messages or adverts sent by SMS, social media, or messaging apps, scammers may share alluring rates.

How do scammers use loan to scam?

Some scammers will instruct their targets to transfer money to them. They may claim that the money is to pay for certain application and processing fees, or taxes/duties that must be paid before the loan can be disbursed. These scammers will disappear without a trace once they have received their targets' money.


How can we avoid getting scammed?

There are 5 different ways.
1. Licence Verification:
Always verify a moneylender's licence before contacting them. Use the official list of authorised moneylenders from the Registry of Moneylenders to confirm their legitimacy.

2. Check for valid contact information:
Look for the moneylender's official contact information from the official list of authorised moneylenders in the Moneyline registry. The list includes their official websites, emails and contact details. A licensed moneylender must maintain a landline for communication.

3. Be careful online:
Online loan offers are convenient, but they can also be risky. Make sure the site is secure (look for "https://" and the padlock icon) and verify the correct contact information. 5. Visit a physical office:
By law, authorised moneylenders cannot disburse loans entirely online because they must personally verify the identity of all borrowers at their approved location. If you come across someone who says they can do business without going to the office, they are probably scammers or unauthorised moneylenders.

4. Protect personal information:
Never share sensitive personal or financial information with unverified sources. Fraudsters can misuse this information for identity theft.

5. Report suspected fraud:
If you suspect loan fraud, report it to the appropriate authorities, such as the police.
